# Where the Next $1,500 Goes in the Roth

**Date:** 9 November 2026
**Question:** Of the 18 Roth holdings (or something better), where does $1,500 more go for maximum
long-term growth?
**Answer:** **VTI**, and only if 2026 earned income supports it. Otherwise the same VTI goes into the
taxable account.

---

## 0. Eligibility comes before the fund

On 10 Sept the record showed **no earned income** for 2026 and **$2,700 already contributed**
(`2026-09-10-no-income-revision.md`). Roth room is the **lesser of $7,500 or earned income**.

To add $1,500 (total $4,200 for 2026):

| Income type | Needed for 2026 |
|---|---|
| W-2 wages | **≥ $4,200** |
| Self-employment / odd jobs (net profit) | **≥ ~$4,925** (`$4,200 ÷ 0.8529`) |

Below that, the extra money is an excess contribution taxed **6% every year** until it's removed.
2026 contributions can be made until **15 April 2027**, so waiting to confirm income costs little.

If the income isn't there: buy VTI in the **taxable** Robinhood account. At ~$0 income, long-term
gains are taxed at 0% federal, so taxable is nearly as good as the Roth for now.

  More in the Roth deepens the largest risk in the book. ETHA at −17% is not a reason to buy.
- **BND** — bonds are the lowest-growth asset; at 23 with a 42-year horizon, Roth space is worth more
  holding stocks. Cash outside the Roth already does the stability job.

- **EEM** — same exposure as VWO at roughly 10× the cost.
- **QUAL / SPMO / VONG / QQQ / SCHD** — reasonable funds, but each is a bet on a slice of the
  large-caps already owned. Momentum (SPMO) and growth (VONG, QQQ) are the slices that ran hardest.
- **CSX, GLD** — single stock and a non-earning asset; neither is the core.

## 3. Why VTI

- Owns every US stock — large, mid and small — in one fund at **0.03%/yr**.
- Already held, so nothing new to track.
- The account's gap is a broad, cheap core; VTI is that core.
- No fund forecast is required. Which tilt wins over the next decade is unknowable; the whole market
  is the bet that doesn't need a forecast.

**Optional split:** if the account should look more like a global index, **$1,100 VTI / $400 VEA**
moves international from ~10% toward ~13%. Either is defensible. VTI alone is the simpler answer.

**Not a better ticker — fewer tickers.** Going forward, new money into **VTI (plus VEA/VWO for
international)** and nothing else. No need to sell anything to get there. In a Roth, a swap like
SPY → VOO would be tax-free, but the cost gap is ~$1.50/yr on the current SPY position. Not worth
a decision.
