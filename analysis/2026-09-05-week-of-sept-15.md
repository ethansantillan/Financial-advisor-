## How much to trust these numbers

### Correcting a premise first

**I am not predicting XRP goes down.** My long-run view is that XRP probably *rises* — I just think
Bitcoin probably rises more, and that 52% in one token is wrong at any price.

The actual numbers I've given: **~60%** XRP clears $1.90 by 2029 (more likely than not — I agreed with
the user). **~25–30%** it reaches $3+. **~25%** it beats Bitcoin. That last one is the recommendation's
basis, and it's a *relative* call. "XRP rises but underperforms" is the base case, not "XRP falls."

### The case for the prediction market

- Across **28,407 Polymarket markets** resolved Jan 2024–May 2026, prices were well calibrated: 40¢
  contracts resolved YES **41%** of the time, 70¢ resolved **72%**. Mean absolute calibration error
  **2.1 percentage points**.
- Same measure: sportsbooks **6.4%**, polling averages **8.9%**. Roughly 3–4× better than polls.
- Called the 2024 presidential winner ahead of national polling.
- On the Fed side, **CME FedWatch predicts FOMC decisions with 88% accuracy 30 days out** vs 75% for raw
  Fed funds futures (*Journal of Futures Markets*, 2025).

### The case against it — which is real

- Vanderbilt's Clinton and Huang examined **2,500+ political markets, $2.5B volume**, across four
  platforms in the final five weeks of the 2024 campaign. **Polymarket beat chance only 67% of the
  time** — vs PredictIt 93%, Kalshi 78%. It was the *worst* of the four.
- Prices for identical contracts **diverged across exchanges**, and arbitrage peaked in the final two
  weeks — the opposite of efficiency.
- Their conclusion: billions of inflow attracted **speculative noise and partisan "cheerleading,"** not
  only informed insiders.
- And this specific contract was at **82% in February, 15% now.** One of those was badly wrong; no
  reason to assume it's the second.

### What I don't need Polymarket for at all

Cloture needs **60**. Republicans hold **53**, expect to lose Hawley, Paul, possibly Tillis → **~50**.
That needs **10+ Democrats**. Exactly **two** have ever voted for it in committee, and **both now oppose
the current text**. Seven Democratic senators signed a statement that it falls short.

**That's arithmetic from the public record, not a market price.** Polymarket at 15% and Galaxy at 10%
are corroboration. Delete both and the whip count says the same thing.

### The strongest argument against my own case

> **CLARITY odds collapsed from ~70% to 15% during exactly the months XRP rose 28–37%.**

August was XRP's best August since 2021, and it happened *while* the legislative case fell apart. If XRP
traded on CLARITY, it would have fallen. It rose — on ETF flows, which hit a record $1.6B.

**Which means XRP may simply not be priced on this bill,** and a failure could hurt far less than
Bernstein's 15–30% altcoin estimate. Analysts are openly debating whether this is a "sell the rumour"
setup — Bitcoin ETFs ran 164% into launch then went flat. If that's right, my event-risk case is
overstated. I can't rule it out.

### What survives

- **The Fed side holds up better than the CLARITY side.** 88% accuracy, a cleaner question, and a hike
  drains liquidity from every risk asset regardless of any bill.
- **The whip count holds regardless of any market.** It's countable.
- **The concentration argument depends on neither.** $100 cash against $24,900 net worth, 52% crypto,
  27% in one token — a problem if CLARITY passes, if it fails, if the Fed hikes or holds, and if XRP
  goes to $3.

**That last line is the honest basis for the recommendation.** Everything else is timing, and timing can
be wrong. Sizing is the argument I'd still make with every probability on this page deleted.

## Where I could be wrong

- **Prediction markets aren't oracles.** 82% in February, 18% now. Thin markets move on small money.
- **Cloture failing isn't the bill dying.** Leadership can renegotiate and refile.
- **The Fed may not hike.** 60–66% isn't 100%, and analysts are split on whether Warsh endorsed a move.
  A hold reads dovish and could rally everything.
- **Bernstein wrote those numbers near BTC $65,000.** It's $77,155 now, so $55–60K is a *steeper* fall
  from here than "10–25%" suggests — closer to −22% to −29%. I used their percentages, which may be
  conservative.
- **It may already be priced.** Markets often sell the rumour and buy the news.
- **My prices are 1–3 days old**, from published pages not a live feed.

None of that changes the direction, because **the plan doesn't need the forecast to be right.** Selling
$3,000 into an emergency fund is correct whether the bill passes, fails, or gets delayed again. The
events only make the timing urgent.
