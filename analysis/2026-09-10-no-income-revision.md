# No Income Changes the Plan

**Date:** 10 September 2026
**New fact:** No annual income. Currently job-hunting, taking occasional odd jobs.
**Effect:** Reverses the Roth recommendation made earlier the same day, and opens a
tax move that is only available while income is zero.

---

## 1. The Roth contribution may be an excess contribution

**You cannot contribute to a Roth IRA without earned income.** The 2026 limit is the
**lesser of $7,500 or your taxable compensation for the year** — not a flat $7,500.
$2,700 is already in there for 2026.

Odd-job money *does* count, but only as **net earnings from self-employment**, and the
self-employment tax reduces it before it counts:

```
compensation = net profit x 0.9235 x 0.9235  ≈  net profit x 0.8529
```

So to legally support the $2,700 already contributed, the odd jobs need roughly
**$3,165 of net profit** in 2026 — not $2,700. (If any of it was W-2 wages instead, the
full wage counts with no reduction.)

**Three cases:**

| 2026 odd-job profit | Excess | 6% penalty per year |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ ~$3,165 | $0 | none — fine |
| ~$1,800 | ~$1,165 | ~$70/yr |
| $0 | $2,700 | **$162/yr, every year it stays** |

The 6% is an annual excise tax that repeats for as long as the excess sits there.

**The fix:** request a **"return of excess contribution"** from Robinhood. They remove the
excess plus the net income attributable to it. Done before the 2026 return deadline —
**15 April 2027**, or 15 October 2027 with an extension — the 6% is avoided entirely.
Robinhood does **not** charge its Early Match Removal Fee on a return of excess, though
the 1% match on the returned amount goes back.

**Action required: tally 2026 odd-job income. That number gates everything else.**

## 2. The Roth recommendation is withdrawn

Earlier today the recommendation was $2,500 into the Roth on the reasoning that 42 years
of compounding there is never taxed. With zero income that reasoning inverts:

- **A Roth shelters tax. There is currently no tax to shelter.** With $0 income, the
  $16,100 standard deduction covers ordinary income and short-term gains, and long-term
  gains fall in the **0% bracket** (0% applies up to $49,450 of taxable income for a
  single filer in 2026). The taxable brokerage account is already tax-free at this
  income level.
- **Contribution room is capped by income he doesn't have.** The room isn't $4,800; it's
  whatever the odd jobs netted, minus the $2,700 already in.
- **Cash matters more without a paycheck.** Roth *contributions* can come out any time,
  but earnings can't, and the account shouldn't be the emergency fund.

**Revised: new money goes to the taxable Robinhood account, not the Roth**, until there
is a job. When employment starts, the Roth becomes the right answer again — and stays
that way for the next forty years.

## 3. The move that only exists right now: tax-gain harvesting

With $0 income, long-term gains are taxed at **0% federal**. That is not a small window —
it is the lowest tax rate he will ever face, and it disappears the day he is hired.

**Sell a long-term winner and rebuy it immediately.** The wash-sale rule applies **only to
losses** (IRC §1091) — selling at a *gain* and repurchasing the same second is fully
permitted, for stocks and for crypto alike. The result: the same position, at a higher
cost basis, for zero federal tax. Every dollar of basis stepped up now is a dollar not
taxed at 15% later.

Applies to anything held **more than one year** and in profit — BTC and SOL are the
candidates, plus META in the taxable account.

**Caveats:**
- **Long-term only** for the 0% rate. Short-term gains are ordinary income (still $0 tax
  under the standard deduction at this income, but the sizing differs).
- **State tax is a separate question.** Many states tax capital gains as ordinary income
  with no 0% band. State unknown — must be checked before executing.
- **Do it before 31 December 2026,** and re-tally if a job starts before then, because
  wages consume the same bracket.
- Execution slippage on the rebuy is the only real cost — seconds of market exposure.

This is the rare recommendation that requires selling nothing, in substance. The position
does not change. Only the basis does.

## 5. The honest consequence: the dilution plan stalls

The plan to fix the XRP concentration without selling relied on **directing new
contributions elsewhere** — roughly $2,000/month, which reached XRP <20% of net worth in
about four months. That plan assumed income. There isn't any.

Without contributions there are only two levers on concentration: **sell, or wait.**
Selling is off the table by his own standing preference. So **XRP stays at ~25.5% of net
worth until there is a paycheck.** That is not a recommendation, it is just what is true.
The concentration risk is unchanged and currently unfixable by any means he'll accept.

**Which makes the job the single highest-value item on the list.** Not a trade — the job.
It restores contributions, restarts the dilution path, and it is worth more than any
allocation decision in this file.
