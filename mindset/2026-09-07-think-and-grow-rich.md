# Think and Grow Rich — The Complete Operating Manual

**Napoleon Hill, 1937. Every principle, every list, every trait — plus how to actually run it.**

*Built 7 September 2026 from the verified 1937 text. Lists below are Hill's own, checked against the
source, not from memory. Commentary and the protocol in Parts 4–7 are mine.*

---

## Part 0 — The frame before the content

### Where the book came from

In 1908, Andrew Carnegie — then the richest man in the world — gave a 25-year-old reporter named
Napoleon Hill an assignment and no salary: go interview the most successful people in America, find
the common denominators, and organize them into a philosophy an ordinary person could follow.
Hill spent 20 years on it. He analyzed **over 500 wealthy men** in depth and, by his account,
**more than 25,000 people who had failed**. *Think and Grow Rich* (1937) is the condensed result,
written at the bottom of the Great Depression.

That origin matters for how you read it. It is not a theory of wealth. It is a **pattern-match across
a few hundred high performers**, written by a man who was himself broke for much of the process.
Some of it is durable psychology that modern research later confirmed under different names. Some of
it is 1937 metaphysics. Part 6 tells you which is which, honestly. Do not skip Part 6.

### "The Secret"

Hill's most famous device: he says the secret of the book is **mentioned at least once in every
chapter, but never named directly** — because "it is more readily recognized by those who are ready
for it," and because something you extract yourself, you keep.

Here is the honest answer, since you asked for everything:

> **The secret is that a definite, burning, emotionalized desire — held in the mind continuously,
> written down, believed as already accomplished, and backed by a plan you refuse to abandon —
> reorganizes your attention, your behavior, and the people you attract, until the outcome becomes
> the most likely thing to happen.**

Hill's compressed version, the single most quoted line in the book:

> *"Whatever the mind of man can conceive and believe, it can achieve."*

And the opening line of the whole book:

> *"Thoughts are things."*

The secret is not that the universe delivers. The secret is that **definiteness is rare, and almost
everyone is running on vague wishes**. When you become one of the few people with an exact target, an
exact price you'll pay, an exact date, and a written plan you read twice a day — you are competing
against a field that mostly hasn't decided what it wants. That is a real, exploitable edge. It's the
whole book.

### How Hill said to read it

Miller Reese Hutchison, Edison's engineer, quoted in the book's own front matter: *"This is not a
novel. It is a textbook on individual achievement… No more than one chapter should be read in a
single night. The reader should underline the sentences which impress him most."*

Hill's own instruction is harder: he says the material "cannot be digested in a week or a month," and
that the principles only work **applied as a set, not cherry-picked**. Reading this document once
does nothing. Running Part 4 for 90 days does something.

## Part 1 — The Thirteen Principles

Hill calls them the thirteen steps to riches. They are sequential in the book but simultaneous in
practice. For each: **what Hill said → the mechanism underneath → what you actually do.**

---

### 1. DESIRE — the starting point of all achievement

*"Wishing will not bring riches. But desiring riches with a state of mind that becomes an obsession,
then planning definite ways and means to acquire riches, and backing those plans with persistence
which does not recognize failure, will bring riches."*

Hill's distinction is between **wishing, wanting, and a burning obsession**. Only the third produces
action under resistance. He opens with Edwin C. Barnes, who wanted to be Edison's *business partner* —
not employee — arrived at the lab as a tramp with no money and no introduction, took a floor-sweeping
job to get proximity, and waited **years**, holding the single objective, until the Edison
Dictating Machine came along that Edison's own salesmen didn't believe in. Barnes sold it and became
Edison's partner. Hill's point: Barnes had **no advantage except definiteness and the refusal of an
alternative**.

Then the counter-example, the most useful story in the book: **R. U. Darby's uncle** struck gold in
Colorado, borrowed money for machinery, mined a rich vein — then the vein vanished. They drilled a
while, quit, and sold the machinery to a junk man for a few hundred dollars. The junk man hired an
engineer, who calculated the vein had faulted. **The gold was three feet from where the Darbys stopped
drilling.** The junk man took millions out of it.

> *"Before success comes in any man's life, he is sure to meet with much temporary defeat, and,
> perhaps, some failure. When defeat overtakes a man, the easiest and most logical thing to do is to
> quit. That is exactly what the majority of men do."*

Darby's second lesson — the "fifty-cent lesson in persistence" — came from watching a sharecropper's
small daughter walk into the mill to demand the fifty cents owed to her mother, refuse to leave, and
stare down Darby's uncle until he paid. She got it because she would not move. Darby went on to become
one of the top life insurance salesmen in America and said his fortune was built on those two lessons.

**Hill's six definite, practical steps** (verbatim in substance, from Chapter 2):

1. **Fix in your mind the exact amount of money you desire.** Not "plenty of money." A number.
2. **Determine exactly what you intend to give in return** for it. *"There is no such reality as
   'something for nothing.'"*
3. **Establish a definite date** by which you intend to possess it.
4. **Create a definite plan, and begin at once, whether you are ready or not**, to put it into action.
5. **Write out a clear, concise statement** of the amount, the deadline, what you will give in return,
   and the plan.
6. **Read your written statement aloud, twice daily** — once on rising, once before sleep. *"As you
   read, see and feel and believe yourself already in possession of the money."*

**Burn the ships.** Hill's other image for desire: Cortez burned his own ships on the shore of Mexico
so his men understood there was no retreat. *"We win — or we perish!"* The general who burns his
boats is not braver; he has **removed the option of a soft landing**, which changes what his people
are willing to do.

> **The mechanism:** Steps 1–5 convert a mood into a specification. Step 6 is spaced repetition of a
> goal with emotional charge attached, which shifts what your attention flags as relevant all day. You
> are not summoning opportunity; you are **installing a filter that stops discarding it**.

> **What you do:** Write the statement today. Not a polished one — today's one. Part 4 has the
> template.

### 2. FAITH — visualization of, and belief in, attainment of desire

*"Faith is the head chemist of the mind."*

Hill's "faith" is **not** religious belief and not optimism. He defines it as a **state of mind
induced deliberately by repetition of instruction to the subconscious** — i.e. it is *manufactured*,
on purpose, by autosuggestion. That's the key line most readers miss:

> *"Faith is a state of mind which may be induced, or created, by affirmation or repeated instructions
> to the subconscious mind, through the principle of auto-suggestion."*

He is explicit that this cuts both ways: the same mechanism that builds confidence builds a
poverty-consciousness in people who repeat defeat to themselves. *"Any impulse of thought which is
repeatedly passed on to the subconscious mind is finally accepted and acted upon."*

His illustration is **Charles M. Schwab's speech** — a talk at a dinner that so persuaded J. P. Morgan
of a steel combine's logic that it led to the formation of **United States Steel**, a $600 million
corporation, out of an idea sold with conviction. Hill's read: the idea existed before; Schwab's
*faith in it, transmitted*, is what created the money.

**THE SELF-CONFIDENCE FORMULA** — write it out, sign it, memorize it, repeat it aloud daily until it's
felt, not recited:

> **First.** I know that I have the ability to achieve the object of my Definite Purpose in life,
> therefore I DEMAND of myself persistent, continuous action toward its attainment, and I here and now
> promise to render such action.
>
> **Second.** I realize the dominating thoughts of my mind will eventually reproduce themselves in
> outward, physical action, and gradually transform themselves into physical reality, therefore I will
> concentrate my thoughts for **thirty minutes daily** upon the task of thinking of the person I intend
> to become, thereby creating in my mind a clear mental picture of that person.
>
> **Third.** I know through the principle of auto-suggestion, any desire that I persistently hold in my
> mind will eventually seek expression through some practical means of attaining the object behind it,
> therefore I will devote **ten minutes daily** to demanding of myself the development of
> SELF-CONFIDENCE.
>
> **Fourth.** I have clearly written down a description of my DEFINITE CHIEF AIM in life, and I will
> never stop trying until I shall have developed sufficient self-confidence for its attainment.
>
> **Fifth.** I fully realize that no wealth or position can long endure unless built upon truth and
> justice, therefore I will engage in no transaction which does not benefit all whom it affects. I will
> succeed by attracting to myself the forces I wish to use, and the cooperation of other people. I will
> induce others to serve me because of my willingness to serve others. I will eliminate hatred, envy,
> jealousy, selfishness and cynicism by developing love for all humanity, because I know that a negative
> attitude toward others can never bring me success. I will cause others to believe in me, because I
> will believe in them, and in myself.

Note what point five is doing. Hill puts an **ethical constraint inside the confidence ritual** — no
transaction that doesn't benefit everyone it touches. That is not decoration. His claim, repeated
throughout, is that the version of this philosophy without it produces short-lived money and a
wrecked person.

Also in this chapter, the poem Hill quotes, worth committing to memory:

> *"If you think you are beaten, you are; / If you think you dare not, you don't. / … Life's battles
> don't always go / To the stronger or faster man; / But soon or late the man who wins / Is the man
> WHO THINKS HE CAN."*

> **The mechanism:** Self-efficacy. Fifty years after Hill, Albert Bandura demonstrated experimentally
> that belief in your own capability is a *causal* input to performance — it changes effort, goal
> difficulty chosen, and persistence after failure — not merely a description of it. Hill got there
> first and stated it as a procedure.

### 3. AUTO-SUGGESTION — the medium for influencing the subconscious mind

The delivery system for #1 and #2. Hill's whole claim in one sentence: **your subconscious acts on
whatever thoughts reach it repeatedly and with emotion attached, and you control the gate.**

The critical qualifier, and the reason most people's affirmations do nothing:

> *"Plain, unemotional words do not influence the subconscious mind. You will get no appreciable
> results until you learn to reach your subconscious mind with thoughts or spoken words which have
> been well emotionalized with BELIEF."*

**Hill's exact instructions:**

- Go somewhere alone, twice a day. Read your written statement **aloud**.
- As you read, **see and feel yourself already in possession** of the thing. Not "I will have" —
  the felt state of already having.
- Do this until you can hold that mental picture on demand.
- Concentration is the skill being trained. *"Concentrate your mind upon the attainment of this
  amount of money, until you can SEE the actual money in your hands."*
- Add the emotion. Desire, faith, and enthusiasm are the carrier wave; the words alone are inert.

> **The mechanism:** Two real effects, stripped of the 1937 vocabulary. (a) **Mental simulation** —
> vividly rehearsing an outcome and the actions to reach it improves follow-through, and rehearsing
> the *process* does more than rehearsing the *trophy*. (b) **Goal priming / selective attention** —
> a goal held with emotional weight changes what you notice and what you count as relevant. The
> caution: visualizing the *outcome alone* can substitute for effort and reduce it. See Part 6 for the
> fix (mental contrasting), which makes this principle strictly better without abandoning it.

### 4. SPECIALIZED KNOWLEDGE — personal experiences or observations

*"There are two kinds of knowledge. One is general, the other is specialized. General knowledge, no
matter how great in quantity or variety it may be, is of but little use in the accumulation of money."*

The hard line Hill draws, which is the whole chapter:

> **"KNOWLEDGE will not attract money, unless it is organized, and intelligently directed, through
> practical PLANS OF ACTION, to the DEFINITE END of accumulation of money."**

Corollary he states directly: *"Knowledge is only potential power. It becomes power only when, and if,
it is organized into definite plans of action, and directed to a definite end."* This is why educated
people are often poor and why "education" — from *educo*, to draw out from within — is not the
collection of facts.

**The Henry Ford story.** During a libel trial Ford was cross-examined with general-knowledge trivia
("How many soldiers did the British send to America to put down the Rebellion of 1776?") to establish
that he was an ignorant man. Ford finally answered:

> *"If I should really WANT to answer the foolish question you have just asked, or any of the other
> questions you have been asking me, let me remind you that I have a row of electric push-buttons on my
> desk, and by pushing the right button, I can summon to my aid men who can answer ANY question I
> desire to ask concerning the business to which I am devoting most of my efforts. Now, will you kindly
> tell me, WHY I should clutter up my mind with general knowledge, for the purpose of being able to
> answer questions, when I have men around me who can supply any knowledge I require?"*

**Practical rules from this chapter:**

- **Decide the specialized knowledge you need before you go get it** — determined by your definite
  purpose, not by curiosity or by what's on offer.
- **You can buy or ally with knowledge you don't have.** A Master Mind of specialists is faster and
  cheaper than becoming a specialist in everything. *"Any man is educated who knows where to get
  knowledge when he needs it, and how to organize that knowledge into definite plans of action."*
- **Continuing education is not optional and not age-limited.** Hill: successful people never stop
  acquiring specialized knowledge related to their major purpose; failures assume school ended it.
- **Sell your specialized knowledge as a service, not as an hour.** He hammers on the person who says
  "I have no opportunity" while sitting on an unpackaged skill.

> **What you do:** Name the *one* domain your definite purpose requires you to be top-decile in within
> 24 months. Write down who already has it. Then buy it (courses, books, a paid mentor) or ally with it
> (Master Mind). Everything else you need, you rent.

### 5. IMAGINATION — the workshop of the mind

*"The imagination is literally the workshop wherein are fashioned all plans created by man. The impulse,
the DESIRE, is given shape, form, and ACTION through the aid of the imaginative faculty of the mind."*

**Two forms, and the distinction is practically useful:**

| | **Synthetic imagination** | **Creative imagination** |
|---|---|---|
| What it does | Rearranges existing concepts, ideas, plans into new combinations | Produces hunches, inspirations, "new" ideas |
| Creates from nothing? | No — works with experience, education, observation | Hill's claim: yes, via the "sixth sense" / Infinite Intelligence |
| When it fires | On demand, any time | Only when the conscious mind is **stimulated by strong desire** |
| Where fortunes come from | **Most of them** | The exceptional ones |
| Gets stronger by | Use | Use — *"it becomes more alert in proportion to its development through USE"* |

Hill is explicit that **most wealth is built by synthetic imagination**: taking two known things and
combining them. Don't wait for lightning.

**The Enchanted Kettle.** An old country doctor sold a kettle, a wooden paddle and a slip of paper with
a secret formula on it to a young pharmacy clerk, **Asa Candler, for $500** — the clerk's entire
savings. The paper was the formula for Coca-Cola. Hill's point: the doctor got $500 for the *stuff*;
the clerk got a fortune because he added **an idea about distribution** to it. The physical ingredients
were nothing. *"The ingredient which does have value is the intangible one — the IDEA."*

**"What I Would Do If I Had a Million Dollars."** Frank Gunsaulus, a young preacher, wanted to found a
school teaching by doing, not theory. He needed $1M and had nothing. He announced a Sunday sermon with
that title, and — sitting down to write it — made a decision: he would raise the money in a week.
Something changed in his voice. After the sermon a stranger walked to the platform, offered his hand,
and said: *"Come to my office tomorrow morning and I will give you the million dollars."* He was Philip
D. Armour. The result is the Armour Institute of Technology (today part of Illinois Tech). Hill's
reading: the money came **after a definite decision with a deadline**, not after the plan was perfect.

> *"Ideas are the beginning points of all fortunes. Ideas are products of the imagination."*

Hill's warning about ideas: *"An idea is an impulse of thought that impels action… ideas have no
value until transformed into definite plans of action."* And: opportunity *"has a sly habit of slipping
in by the back door, and often it comes disguised in the form of misfortune or temporary defeat."*

> **What you do:** Keep an idea capture file. Every week, force **ten** synthetic combinations against
> your definite purpose — "X applied to Y", "the model of A in the market of B." Nine will be garbage.
> The exercise is the point; the faculty strengthens by use.

### 6. ORGANIZED PLANNING — the crystallization of desire into action

The longest and most practical chapter in the book. Desire, faith and imagination are inert without
this one.

**Hill's plan-building instructions:**

1. Ally yourself with a **Master Mind group** — as many people as you need for the creation and
   execution of your plan.
2. Decide **what you will give each member** in return, before you ask. Nobody works indefinitely for
   nothing.
3. Meet with them **at least twice a week, more if possible**, until you've jointly perfected the plan.
4. Maintain **perfect harmony** with every member. Failure here is failure everywhere.

And the sentence to tattoo somewhere:

> **"If the first plan which you adopt does not work successfully, replace it with a new plan; if this
> new plan fails to work, replace it, in turn with still another, and so on, until you find a plan
> which DOES work. Right here is the point at which the majority of men meet with failure, because of
> their lack of PERSISTENCE in creating new plans to take the place of those which fail."**

> *"TEMPORARY DEFEAT should mean only one thing, the certain knowledge that there is something wrong
> with your plan."* Not with you. With **the plan**. Separating those two is most of emotional
> durability.

And: *"No man is ever whipped, until he QUITS — in his own mind."*

#### The 11 major attributes of leadership

1. **UNWAVERING COURAGE** — based on knowledge of self and of one's occupation.
2. **SELF-CONTROL** — *"The man who cannot control himself can never control others."*
3. **A KEEN SENSE OF JUSTICE** — without fairness you cannot command or retain respect.
4. **DEFINITENESS OF DECISION** — a man who wavers shows he is not sure of himself.
5. **DEFINITENESS OF PLANS** — *"plan his work, and work his plan."* Guesswork is a ship without a
   rudder.
6. **THE HABIT OF DOING MORE THAN PAID FOR** — the leader must out-give what he asks of others.
7. **A PLEASING PERSONALITY** — *"No slovenly, careless person can become a successful leader."*
8. **SYMPATHY AND UNDERSTANDING** — be in sympathy with your people and understand their problems.
9. **MASTERY OF DETAIL** — of the details of your own position.
10. **WILLINGNESS TO ASSUME FULL RESPONSIBILITY** — for the mistakes and shortcomings of followers.
    *"If one of his followers makes a mistake… the leader must consider that it is HE who failed."*
11. **COOPERATION** — leadership calls for power, and power calls for cooperation.

Hill adds: there are two kinds of leadership — **by consent** (effective, durable) and **by force**
(never endures). *"People will not follow forced leadership indefinitely."*

#### The 10 major causes of failure in leadership

> *"It is just as essential to know WHAT NOT TO DO as it is to know what to do."*

1. **Inability to organize details.** No real leader is ever "too busy." Being too busy to change your
   plans or handle an emergency is an admission of inefficiency. (The fix is delegation, not heroics.)
2. **Unwillingness to render humble service.** Great leaders will do any job they'd ask of another.
3. **Expectation of pay for what they "know" instead of what they DO with what they know.**
4. **Fear of competition from followers.** *"The leader who fears that one of his followers may take
   his position is practically sure to realize that fear."* The able leader trains understudies —
   the only way to multiply yourself.
5. **Lack of imagination.** Can't meet emergencies, can't create plans.
6. **Selfishness.** *"The really great leader CLAIMS NONE OF THE HONORS."* Most people work harder for
   recognition than for money.
7. **Intemperance.** Followers lose respect for a leader without control of appetites.
8. **Disloyalty.** *"Disloyalty marks one as being less than the dust of the earth."* Hill calls this
   the cause of failure in every walk of life.
9. **Emphasis of the "authority" of leadership.** Leading by fear is force, not leadership.
10. **Emphasis of title.** *"The doors to the office of the real leader are open to all who wish to
    enter."* Competent leaders need no title to get respect.

#### The QQS formula for selling your services

**QUALITY + QUANTITY + SPIRIT = perfect salesmanship of service.**

- **QUALITY** — performing every detail of your position in the most efficient way possible, with
  greater efficiency always in mind.
- **QUANTITY** — the *habit* of rendering all the service you're capable of, at all times, and
  increasing the amount as skill grows.
- **SPIRIT** — the *habit* of agreeable, harmonious conduct that induces cooperation.

Carnegie stressed the third hardest: he would not keep a man, however great the quantity or quality
of his work, who could not work in **harmony**. Read that again if you are the smart, difficult one.

Paired with this is Hill's **"doing more than paid for"** doctrine — render more and better service
than compensated for, consistently, and you (a) become the obvious promotion and (b) are the last one
cut. He frames it as the law by which you *raise your own price*, not as martyrdom.

#### The 30 major causes of failure — the self-audit

Hill's instruction: read it **while checking yourself against each line**, and have someone who knows
you well check you too, because you cannot see all of these in yourself.

1. Unfavorable **hereditary background** (the only one he calls hard to fix — offset it with a Master
   Mind)
2. **Lack of a well-defined purpose in life**
3. **Lack of ambition to aim above mediocrity**
4. **Insufficient education** (overcome-able; *"educated" means developed from within, not schooled*)
5. **Lack of self-discipline** — *"you must control your appetites… conquer self before you conquer
   others"*
6. **Ill health** (most causes are within your control: overeating, wrong thinking, wrong use of sex,
   lack of exercise, poor breathing)
7. **Unfavorable environmental influences during childhood**
8. **Procrastination** — *"one of the most common causes of failure"*
9. **Lack of persistence** — *"most of us are good starters but poor finishers"*
10. **Negative personality**
11. **Lack of controlled sexual urge** — the most powerful energy; must be transmuted, not spent
12. **Uncontrolled desire for "something for nothing"** — the gambling instinct
13. **Lack of a well-defined power of decision** — *"men who succeed reach decisions promptly"*
14. **One or more of the six basic fears** (Part 2)
15. **Wrong selection of a mate in marriage**
16. **Over-caution** — *"the person who takes no chances generally has to take whatever is left"*
17. **Wrong selection of associates in business**
18. **Superstition and prejudice** — superstition is a form of fear, and a sign of ignorance
19. **Wrong selection of a vocation** — you cannot succeed at work you don't like
20. **Lack of concentration of effort** — the jack-of-all-trades
21. **The habit of indiscriminate spending**
22. **Lack of enthusiasm**
23. **Intolerance** — the closed mind stops growing
24. **Intemperance** — chiefly eating, drink, and sexual activities
25. **Inability to cooperate with others** — *"more people lose their positions and their big
    opportunities in life because of this fault than for all other reasons combined"*
26. **Possession of power not acquired through self-effort** — inherited money is *"often fatal"*
27. **Intentional dishonesty**
28. **Egotism and vanity**
29. **Guessing instead of thinking** — most people are too lazy to get facts to think accurately with
30. **Lack of capital**
31. *(Hill's own #31)* — **"Under this, name any particular cause of failure from which you have
    suffered that has not been included in the foregoing."**

That last line is the most important one on the list. Fill it in.

#### Marketing your personal services

Hill's practical block on this: prepare a written **brief** of your services as if you were selling a
security — education, experience, what you will do, references, and a *specific proposal for what you
will accomplish in the position*, not a request for a job. He says almost nobody does this and that it
is the single cheapest edge in the labor market. It still is; it is now called a "value proposition"
and a "90-day plan," and it still separates candidates.

Also here: **"the capital"** — his argument that the biggest asset available to anyone with an idea is
organized capital and organized cooperation, available to those who bring a definite plan.

### 7. DECISION — the mastery of procrastination

*"ACCURATE analysis of over 25,000 men and women who had experienced failure, disclosed the fact that
LACK OF DECISION was near the head of the list of the 30 major causes of FAILURE."*

The finding, stated as a rule:

> **"Analysis of several hundred people who had accumulated fortunes well beyond the million dollar
> mark, disclosed the fact that every one of them had the habit of REACHING DECISIONS PROMPTLY, and of
> changing these decisions SLOWLY, if, and when they were changed. People who fail to accumulate money,
> without exception, have the habit of reaching decisions, if at all, very slowly, and of changing
> these decisions quickly and often."**

Read that as a diagnostic you can run on yourself this week. Which pattern is yours?

**Keep your own counsel.** Hill is emphatic and slightly ruthless about this:

- *"Tell the world what you intend to do, but first SHOW IT."*
- Opinions are the cheapest commodity on earth. Everyone has a stock ready to hand you. If you are
  influenced by opinions when you make decisions, **you will not succeed in any undertaking**.
- Friends and relatives, *"without intending to do so… often handicap one through 'opinions,'
  sometimes through ridicule, which is meant to be humorous."*
- Take no one into your confidence except the members of your Master Mind — and choose those for
  **complete sympathy and harmony with your purpose**.
- *"Close your mouth and open your ears."* Genuinely intelligent people, he notes, are known to be so
  by how much they *listen* and how little they need to be seen to know.
- **The Henry Ford example**: Ford decided to keep producing the Model T long past when everyone
  including his own advisers told him to change. He changed too slowly, arguably — but Hill's point is
  that the same trait that made that mistake made the fortune. *He was a man who knew his own mind.*

**Freedom or death on a decision.** Hill closes the chapter with the signing of the Declaration of
Independence — a decision, made by 56 men who knew signing meant hanging if it failed, that *"gave to
every American the power to control his own destiny."* His reading: history's biggest turns are
decisions taken with the escape route closed, by people who accepted the price in advance.

> **What you do:** Two habits, this week. (1) **Default to deciding at the moment you have ~70% of the
> information** you'd like, then don't relitigate for a fixed period. (2) **Stop broadcasting.** Take
> your plans off social, out of group chats, away from people who "just want you to be realistic."
> Announce results, not intentions.

### 8. PERSISTENCE — the sustained effort necessary to induce faith

*"PERSISTENCE is an essential factor in the procedure of transmuting DESIRE into its monetary
equivalent. The basis of persistence is the POWER OF WILL."*

Hill's core claim: persistence is **not a personality trait you have or lack**. It is a state of mind,
therefore it can be **cultivated** — and it is built out of eight ingredients, all of which you can
work on directly.

**The eight factors persistence is built from:**

1. **Definiteness of purpose** — knowing what you want is the first and most important step.
2. **Desire** — it is comparatively easy to acquire persistence in pursuit of an intense desire.
3. **Self-reliance** — belief in your ability to carry out a plan.
4. **Definiteness of plans** — organized plans, even weak ones, encourage persistence.
5. **Accurate knowledge** — knowing your plans are sound, based on experience or observation;
   *guessing* destroys persistence.
6. **Cooperation** — sympathy, understanding, harmonious cooperation.
7. **Will-power** — the habit of concentrating thoughts on a definite purpose.
8. **Habit** — *"Persistence is the direct result of habit."* Fear can be cured by forced repetition
   of courageous acts.

#### The 16 symptoms of lack of persistence — Hill's diagnostic

Go down this list honestly. Check the ones that are yours.

1. Failure to recognize and clearly define exactly what one wants.
2. Procrastination, with or without cause — *"usually backed up with a formidable array of alibis and
   excuses."*
3. Lack of interest in acquiring specialized knowledge.
4. Indecision — the habit of "passing the buck" instead of facing issues squarely.
5. The habit of **relying on alibis** instead of creating definite plans for solving problems.
6. **Self-satisfaction.** *"There is but little remedy for this affliction, and no hope for those who
   suffer from it."*
7. **Indifference** — usually reflected in readiness to compromise rather than meet opposition.
8. The habit of **blaming others** for one's mistakes, and accepting unfavorable circumstances as
   unavoidable.
9. **Weakness of desire** — due to neglect in the choice of MOTIVES that impel action.
10. **Willingness, even eagerness, to quit at the first sign of defeat** — based on one or more of the
    six basic fears.
11. **Lack of organized plans, placed in writing where they may be analyzed.**
12. The habit of **neglecting to move on ideas**, or to grasp opportunity when it appears.
13. **Wishing instead of willing.**
14. The habit of **compromising with poverty** instead of aiming at riches; general absence of ambition
    to be, to do, and to own.
15. **Searching for shortcuts to riches** — trying to GET without GIVING a fair equivalent; usually
    reflected in gambling.
16. **Fear of criticism** — failing to create and act on plans because of what other people will think,
    do, or say. Hill calls this one *"at the head of the list"* because it usually operates in the
    subconscious, where you don't notice it.

#### The four steps to the persistence habit

> *"They call for no great amount of intelligence, no particular amount of education, and but little
> time or effort."*

1. **A definite purpose backed by burning desire for its fulfillment.**
2. **A definite plan, expressed in continuous action.**
3. **A mind closed tightly against all negative and discouraging influences** — including the negative
   suggestions of relatives, friends and acquaintances.
4. **A friendly alliance with one or more persons who will encourage you to follow through** with both
   plan and purpose.

Hill says the entire purpose of all thirteen principles is to make these four steps automatic. If you
take one thing from the book operationally, take these four. They are, in modern terms: a goal, a
system, an environment, and accountability.

**Persistence stories:** Fannie Hurst collected **36 rejection slips** from the *Saturday Evening Post*
before her first sale and worked for four years on Broadway "with the doors of failure closed in her
face." Kate Smith "sang for years without a price" until persistence made her a star. Hill's most
useful observation about grinding:

> *"There is a mysterious power which comes to the aid of those who fight on in the face of
> discouragement… the hidden Guide who tests men through all sorts of discouraging experiences. Those
> who pick themselves up after defeat and keep on trying, arrive."*

Strip the mysticism and the observation still holds: **the number of people still trying at month 18
is a tiny fraction of the number who started, and the field thins faster than the difficulty rises.**

> *"Riches do not respond to wishes. They respond only to definite plans, backed by definite desires,
> through constant PERSISTENCE."*

### 9. THE MASTER MIND — the driving force

Hill's definition:

> **"The Master Mind may be defined as: 'Coordination of knowledge and effort, in a spirit of harmony,
> between two or more people, for the attainment of a definite purpose.'"**

Note the load-bearing phrase: **in a spirit of harmony**. A group of people who don't get along is not
a Master Mind; Hill says it is worse than nothing, because friction consumes the energy the alliance
was supposed to produce.

He says it works on two levels:

- **The economic feature** — obvious: you get the advice, counsel, experience, education, specialized
  knowledge and personal cooperation of other people. Very few fortunes were built without it.
- **The psychic feature** — his metaphysical claim: when two minds coordinate in harmony, a "third
  mind" forms, an intangible force greater than the sum. He compares it to batteries in series: more
  cells, more energy.

**The evidence he cites:** Andrew Carnegie's staff of about **fifty men** he'd surrounded himself with
for the definite purpose of making and marketing steel — Carnegie attributed his entire fortune to that
alliance, not to his own knowledge of steel. Henry Ford's rise "from poverty, illiteracy, and
ignorance" tracked, in Hill's telling, his friendships with **Edison, Burbank, Burroughs and
Firestone** — *"men blend mental forces… a man takes on the nature and the habits and the power of
thought of those with whom he associates."*

> *"POVERTY and RICHES often change places."*

**The applied lesson is about your environment, and it is brutal:** you are running an average of the
five to ten minds you're in contact with most. Hill's version — *"a man takes on the nature and habits
and power of thought of those with whom he associates in a spirit of sympathy and harmony."*

> **What you do:** Build one. 3–6 people, definite shared purpose or committed mutual support, meets on
> a fixed schedule (Hill says twice a week; weekly is realistic), everyone gets something concrete out
> of it, and you enforce harmony ruthlessly — one cynic destroys the asset. This is Persistence step 4
> and it is the single highest-leverage thing in the book that you have not already been told a hundred
> times.

### 10. SEX TRANSMUTATION — the mystery

The most misread chapter. Hill's argument is **not** abstinence and is not primarily about sex at all —
it is about **the strongest drive you have and where you point it**.

> *"The meaning of the word 'transmute' is, in simple language, 'the changing, or transferring of one
> element, or form of energy, into another.' The emotion of sex brings into being a state of mind…
> Sex desire is the most powerful of human desires."*

He notes the drive makes people willing to risk life and reputation, and says that energy, **redirected
into a definite purpose**, produces imagination, courage, will-power, persistence and creative ability
"unknown at other times." Suppression doesn't work; discharge alone wastes it; **redirection** is the
skill.

#### The ten mind stimuli

*"The human mind responds to stimuli, through which it may be 'keyed up' to high rates of vibration,
known as enthusiasm, creative imagination, intense desire, etc."*

1. The **desire for sex expression**
2. **Love**
3. A burning desire for **fame, power, or financial gain — money**
4. **Music**
5. **Friendship** — between those of the same or the opposite sex
6. **A Master Mind alliance** — harmony between two or more allied for advancement
7. **Mutual suffering** — such as that experienced by people who are persecuted
8. **Auto-suggestion**
9. **Fear**
10. **Narcotics and alcohol**

**Eight are natural and constructive. Two — 9 and 10 — are destructive.** Hill lists them so you can
see your own sources of drive plainly and choose from the top of the list rather than the bottom. Sex
desire heads the list as the most intense.

**Why success often comes after forty.** Hill's data claim: men rarely do their best work before 40,
and usually strike their stride between **40 and 60** — because before that they typically **dissipate**
sex energy through overindulgence and haven't learned to redirect it. He calls the years 30–40 the
period when men "slowly learn this art." He explicitly says this isn't moralism: *"nature's most
beautiful emotion"* is not the problem; **unchanneled expenditure** is.

**Love, romance and sex — the triple triangle.** Hill's most humane passage:

- **Sex alone** drives to physical action and can be purely biological.
- **Love** tempers and directs it — *"Love is the safety valve"* — and blends into spirituality.
- **Romance** sustains it over years.
- The three together produce the mind of a genius. *"The emotion of love… slows down, and modifies the
  nature of sex energy… Love, Romance and Sex are all emotions capable of driving men to heights of
  super achievement. Love is the emotion which serves as a safety valve, and insures balance, poise,
  and constructive effort."*
- He is blunt that **a person's partner can make or break them**, and that a man whose home is
  harmonious can carry loads that would break him otherwise. *"Remember this, if you wish to become a
  master of salesmanship: your wife can make you or break you."* (Written in 1937 to men; read it as
  "your partner," and read it in both directions.)
- On jealousy: *"Jealousy, and other similar forms of insanity"* destroy this asset. Watch for it.

Hill also names achievers he attributes to highly-sexed natures channelled into work — Lincoln,
Shakespeare, Napoleon, Jefferson, Washington, Emerson, Burns, Elbert Hubbard, Woodrow Wilson — and
argues Lincoln's greatness *awoke* after his love for Anne Rutledge.

> **What you do, in modern terms:** Notice that the days you're most driven are days you have high
> energy and something to prove. Don't burn that state on scrolling, porn, gambling, or a party you
> won't remember. **Build a channel** — the moment you feel the charge, it goes into the work, the
> training session, the pitch, the deep block. Point 10 on the list (narcotics and alcohol) is the
> lazy substitute for point 3, and it costs you the next day too.

### 11. THE SUBCONSCIOUS MIND — the connecting link

*"The subconscious mind works day and night. Through methods of procedure unknown to man, the
subconscious mind draws upon the forces of Infinite Intelligence for the power with which it
voluntarily transmutes one's desires into their physical equivalent."*

Hill's realistic caveat, which he does state: **you cannot entirely control your subconscious, but you
can voluntarily hand it any plan, desire or purpose you wish transformed.** The gate is what you feed
it. And critically: *"it will not remain idle. If you fail to plant DESIRES in your subconscious mind,
it will feed upon the thoughts which reach it as the result of your neglect."* An unmanaged mind runs
on whatever's ambient — which in 2026 means an algorithm chose it.

**The seven major positive emotions** — the carriers that get thoughts through:

**DESIRE · FAITH · LOVE · SEX · ENTHUSIASM · ROMANCE · HOPE**

**The seven major negative emotions** — to be avoided:

**FEAR · JEALOUSY · HATRED · REVENGE · GREED · SUPERSTITION · ANGER**

> **"POSITIVE AND NEGATIVE EMOTIONS CANNOT OCCUPY THE MIND AT THE SAME TIME. One or the other must
> dominate. It is your responsibility to make sure that positive emotions constitute the dominating
> influence of your mind."**

He adds a habit-formation note that is genuinely good advice: *"Here the law of HABIT will come to your
aid. Form the habit of applying and using the positive emotions! Eventually, they will dominate your
mind so completely, that the negatives cannot enter it."* Not suppression — **crowding out by
occupancy**.

His practical instruction: *"Mix emotion with your affirmations."* Words alone don't get through; the
seven positives are the delivery vehicle.

### 12. THE BRAIN — a broadcasting and receiving station for thought

Hill's most speculative chapter. His model:

- **The subconscious mind** = the **sending station** of the brain.
- **Creative imagination** = the **receiving set**.
- **Auto-suggestion** = the agency by which you **put the station into operation**.

He claims thought "vibrates" and can pass between minds — telepathy and thought-transference — and
that the Master Mind's power partly comes from this. He references his own experiments and Alexander
Graham Bell and Elmer Gates' work on the "ether."

**Where this chapter is actually useful, without buying the physics:** the operational instruction is
that **you cannot receive an insight while your mind is full of noise and fear.** Hill's practical
prescription is deliberate solitude and stimulation of the mind through the strongest emotions. That
is a real, useful, testable practice: **quiet, undistracted, emotionally engaged thinking time is where
non-obvious ideas actually arrive** — and almost nobody schedules it.

Note the honest read: modern evidence does not support thought-transference. It *does* support that
insight arrives during incubation and low-stimulation states rather than during grinding. Take the
practice, leave the mechanism.

### 13. THE SIXTH SENSE — the door to the temple of wisdom

*"The thirteenth principle is known as the SIXTH SENSE, through which Infinite Intelligence may, and
will communicate voluntarily, without any effort from, or demands by, the individual."*

Hill is careful here: he says this principle **cannot be assimilated or used until the other twelve
have been mastered**, and that it comes to you as "hunches" and "inspirations" from the creative
imagination — the "guardian angel" that opens the door to the temple of wisdom.

**The technique that is genuinely worth stealing: the Invisible Counselors.**

Hill rebuilt his own character deliberately. Every night, for years, he held an **imaginary council
meeting** with nine men whose lives had most impressed him: **Emerson, Paine, Edison, Darwin, Lincoln,
Burbank, Napoleon, Ford and Carnegie.** He sat as chairman, addressed each in turn, and asked each for
the specific quality he wanted from them — for example, asking Emerson for an understanding of nature,
Napoleon for self-reliance and the ability to inspire, Lincoln for patience and a keen sense of justice,
Carnegie for the ability to organize.

His stated purpose: *"to rebuild my own character so it would represent a composite of the characters
of my imaginary counselors."* He calls it **voluntary rebirth**.

Hill adds the disclaimer explicitly — he knows they are figures of his imagination, and warns the
reader against literal belief while insisting the practice reshaped him.

> **Why this works, in plain terms:** you already model other people's minds constantly. Deliberately
> simulating a specific admired person's judgment gives you access to a *different* decision procedure
> than your default one — it breaks you out of your own habitual framing. It's the "what would X do"
> heuristic, systematized, nightly, with a written record.

> **What you do:** Pick 5–9 people (dead, living, personal, famous — the utility is in the *specificity
> of the trait*, not the fame). Name the exact quality you want from each. Convene them for 10 minutes,
> in writing, on any hard decision. This is the most underrated practice in the book.

## Part 2 — The Six Ghosts of Fear (and the seventh evil)

*"Before you can put ANY part of this philosophy into successful use, your mind must be prepared to
receive it. The preparation is not difficult. It begins with study, analysis, and understanding of
three enemies which you shall have to clear out. These are INDECISION, DOUBT, and FEAR."*

Hill's sequence: **indecision crystallizes into doubt, the two blend into fear.** They "blend"
gradually and, once formed, operate below awareness — which is why he insists on a written self-audit
rather than introspection.

He also states plainly: **fears are nothing more than states of mind, and states of mind are subject
to control and direction.**

### The six basic fears, in order of how commonly they do damage

| # | Fear | What it actually costs you |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **POVERTY** | The most destructive. Kills initiative, imagination, enthusiasm, self-reliance, definiteness of purpose |
| 2 | **CRITICISM** | Kills initiative and ambition; the reason most people never start |
| 3 | **ILL HEALTH** | Ties to fears of poverty and old age; produces the thing it fears |
| 4 | **LOSS OF LOVE OF SOMEONE** | Jealousy; the most painful, per Hill |
| 5 | **OLD AGE** | Fear of poverty in old age + fear of diminishing capacity |
| 6 | **DEATH** | Diminishes as understanding grows; often bound up with the others |

Hill's line on the first: *"There can be no compromise between POVERTY and RICHES. The two roads that
lead to poverty and riches travel in opposite directions."* And: *"Man's helplessness against poverty
is the reason he fears it most."*

### The symptoms — Hill's diagnostic checklists

**Symptoms of the fear of POVERTY:**
- **INDIFFERENCE** — lack of ambition, willingness to tolerate poverty, accepting whatever life offers
  without protest, mental and physical laziness
- **INDECISION** — permitting others to do one's thinking; staying "on the fence"
- **DOUBT** — expressed through alibis and excuses to cover, explain away, or apologize for failure;
  sometimes as envy of the successful, or criticism of them
- **WORRY** — finding fault with others, spending beyond income, neglect of personal appearance,
  scowling and frowning, intemperance, nervousness, self-consciousness
- **OVER-CAUTION** — looking for the negative in every circumstance, thinking and talking of possible
  failure instead of concentrating on success, knowing all the roads to disaster and none to avoiding
  it, waiting for "the right time" until waiting becomes a permanent habit, pessimism
- **PROCRASTINATION** — *"putting off until tomorrow that which should have been done last year"*;
  spending enough time creating alibis to have done the job; compromising with difficulties instead of
  harnessing them

**Symptoms of the fear of CRITICISM:**
- **SELF-CONSCIOUSNESS** — nervousness, timidity in conversation and meeting strangers, awkward
  movement of hands and limbs, shifting eyes
- **LACK OF POISE** — poor voice control, nervousness around others, poor posture, poor memory
- **PERSONALITY** — lacking firmness of decision, personal charm, and the ability to state opinions
  definitely; side-stepping issues instead of meeting them; agreeing without examining
- **INFERIORITY COMPLEX** — expressing self-approval by word and action to cover a feeling of
  inferiority; using "big words," imitating others in dress and speech; boasting of imaginary
  achievements
- **EXTRAVAGANCE** — keeping up with the Joneses; spending beyond income
- **LACK OF INITIATIVE** — failing to embrace opportunities for self-advancement; fear of expressing
  opinions; evasiveness in speech and manner; deceit in both
- **LACK OF AMBITION** — mental and physical laziness, lack of self-assertion, slowness in reaching
  decisions, easily influenced by others, the habit of criticizing others behind their backs and
  flattering them to their faces

Hill's blunt note on this fear: parents do irreparable damage to children through criticism, and it is
*"the fear which makes men and women follow the crowd."* Almost nobody is willing to be thought a fool
for long enough to become excellent at something.

**Symptoms of the fear of ILL HEALTH:**
- **AUTO-SUGGESTION** — negative self-suggestion; looking for and expecting to find symptoms of disease
- **HYPOCHONDRIA** — talking of illness, concentrating the mind on disease, expecting its appearance
- **EXERCISE** — fear of ill health interfering with proper physical exercise, resulting in overweight
- **SUSCEPTIBILITY** — the fear breaking down body resistance and creating conditions for illness
- **SELF-CODDLING** — making a bid for sympathy using imaginary illness as the lure
- **INTEMPERANCE** — using alcohol or narcotics to destroy pain instead of eliminating the cause

**Symptoms of the fear of LOSS OF LOVE:**
- **JEALOUSY** — suspicion of friends and loved ones without reasonable evidence; *"a form of dementia
  praecox"*
- **FAULT FINDING** — with friends, relatives, associates and loved ones on the slightest provocation
- **GAMBLING** — gambling, stealing, cheating and otherwise taking hazardous chances to provide money
  for loved ones, believing love can be bought

**Symptoms of the fear of OLD AGE:**
- Slowing down and developing an inferiority complex **at around forty**, falsely believing one is
  "slipping" — when in fact those are the years of greatest wisdom and understanding
- Apologizing for one's age instead of being grateful for it
- Killing off initiative, imagination and self-reliance by assuming one is too old
- Dressing and acting the part of a much younger person, affecting youth

**Symptoms of the fear of DEATH:**
- The habit of **thinking about dying instead of making the most of life** — generally due to lack of
  purpose or a suitable occupation
- Most common in the aged, but sometimes in the young; the best remedy is a **burning desire for
  achievement**, backed by useful service to others

### OLD MAN WORRY

Hill treats worry as fear's sustained form: *"Worry is a state of mind based upon fear. It works slowly,
but persistently. It is insidious and subtle. Step by step it 'digs itself in' until it paralyzes one's
reasoning faculty, destroys self-confidence and initiative."*

His remedy is not comfort — it's **decision**:

> *"Worry is a form of sustained fear caused by indecision, therefore it is a state of mind which can
> be controlled. An unsettled mind is helpless. Indecision makes an unsettled mind."*

You kill worry by **deciding**. A decision, even a mediocre one, ends the loop.

### THE SEVENTH BASIC EVIL — susceptibility to negative influences

Hill's own emphasis, and the most important paragraph in the last chapter:

> *"In addition to the Six Basic Fears, there is another evil… It is so subtle that its presence often
> is not detected. This affliction cannot properly be classed as a fear. **IT IS MORE DEEPLY SEATED AND
> MORE OFTEN FATAL THAN ALL OF THE SIX FEARS.** For want of a better name, let us call this evil
> SUSCEPTIBILITY TO NEGATIVE INFLUENCES."*
>
> *"Men who accumulate great riches always protect themselves against this evil! The poverty stricken
> never do!"*

**How to protect yourself against negative influences — Hill's list:**

- Recognize that **you have will-power**, and put it into constant use until it builds a wall of
  immunity in your own mind.
- Recognize that you, and every other human being, are **by nature lazy, indifferent, and susceptible
  to all suggestions which harmonize with your weaknesses**. (Hill is unusually honest here: the
  defence is structural, not a matter of being a good person.)
- Recognize that you are by nature susceptible to all six basic fears, and **set up habits to
  counteract them**.
- Recognize that negative influences often work through the subconscious, are therefore hard to detect,
  and **keep your mind closed against all people who depress or discourage you in any way**.
- Clean out your medicine chest; stop pandering to imaginary illness.
- **Deliberately seek the company of people who influence you to THINK AND ACT FOR YOURSELF.**
- **Do not EXPECT trouble** — *"it has a tendency not to disappoint."*

> *"Without doubt, the most common weakness of all human beings is the habit of leaving their minds
> open to the negative influence of other people."*

In 2026 the "negative influences" mostly arrive through a screen, algorithmically selected for
emotional reaction, several hundred times a day. Hill's defence — an audited input diet — is more
necessary now than when he wrote it, not less.

### "Fifty-Seven Famous Alibis" — by Old Man IF

Hill closes the book with a long catalogue of the excuses he collected: *IF I didn't have a wife and
family… IF I had money… IF I had a good education… IF I could get a job… IF I had time… IF other people
understood me… IF I had been given a chance… IF I were only younger… IF I could do what I want…
IF I had been born rich… IF I dared assert myself…* and, the one he calls the most damning:

> *"IF I had dared to face the truth about myself, I would have found out what is wrong with me, and
> corrected it, then I might have had a chance to profit by my mistakes and learn something from the
> experience of others, for I know that there is something WRONG with me, or I would now be where I
> WOULD HAVE BEEN IF I had spent more time analyzing my weaknesses, and less time building alibis to
> cover them."*

His verdict on alibi-building: *"The building of alibis is a deeply rooted habit. Habits are difficult
to break, especially when they provide justification for something we do. Plato had this truth in mind
when he said, 'The first and best victory is to conquer self. To be conquered by self is, of all things,
the most shameful and vile.'"*

And the closing challenge: *"Why not discard all alibis, and get down to business?"*

## Part 3 — The complete trait profile

Everything Hill says about the character of the person who succeeds, consolidated into one list. This
is the "become the best version of yourself" section — the traits are the deliverable; the money is
the side effect.

### The 12 traits Hill treats as non-negotiable

| # | Trait | Hill's test for whether you have it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Definiteness of purpose** | Can you state, in one sentence, exactly what you want, by when, and what you'll give for it? |
| 2 | **Decisiveness** | Do you decide promptly and change slowly — or decide slowly and change often? |
| 3 | **Persistence** | When plan #1 fails, do you build plan #2 the same week? |
| 4 | **Self-discipline / self-control** | Can you control appetites, temper, tongue, spending, attention? *"Conquer self before you conquer others."* |
| 5 | **Self-reliance** | Do you need consensus before you act? |
| 6 | **Accurate thinking** | Do you get facts, or guess? Do you distinguish *information* from *fact*? |
| 7 | **Enthusiasm** | Can you transmit conviction? Nobody buys from the unconvinced. |
| 8 | **Pleasing personality / harmony** | Would Carnegie keep you? (He'd fire brilliance that couldn't work agreeably.) |
| 9 | **Going the extra mile** | Do you render more and better service than you're paid for, as a *habit*? |
| 10 | **Imagination, used deliberately** | Do you generate combinations on purpose, or wait for inspiration? |
| 11 | **Tolerance and an open mind** | *"The person with a closed mind on any subject seldom gets ahead."* |
| 12 | **Integrity under pressure** | Would you take the deal that benefits you and hurts someone it touches? |

### The traits Hill says are fatal, in his own emphasis

**Self-satisfaction** (he says there is "no hope" for it) · **the alibi habit** · **blaming others** ·
**indecision** · **fear of criticism** · **wishing instead of willing** · **searching for shortcuts** ·
**compromising with poverty** · **guessing instead of thinking** · **inability to cooperate** ·
**disloyalty** · **egotism and vanity** · **intemperance** · **quitting at the first sign of defeat**.

### The eight things Hill says money will not survive without

1. Definiteness of purpose · 2. A plan · 3. Persistence · 4. A Master Mind · 5. Specialized knowledge ·
6. Harmony with others · 7. Doing more than paid for · 8. Justice — *no transaction that doesn't benefit
everyone it affects.*

### Hill's law of compensation, stated plainly

You get paid for **what you do with what you know**, and you can raise your own price by two levers
only: **the quality/quantity/spirit of what you render** (QQS), and **the rarity of the specialized
knowledge you organize into plans**. Everything else in the book is about generating the persistence
to keep pulling those two levers past the point where most people stop.

## Part 4 — The protocol: how to actually run this

Reading the book changes nothing. This is the executable version. It takes about **50 minutes a day**,
most of which you can stack onto things you already do.

### Step 1 — Write your Definite Chief Aim (today, 30 minutes)

One page. Handwritten is better — the friction is part of it. Use this exact structure (it is Hill's
six steps, in order, in a form you can read aloud):

```
                        MY DEFINITE CHIEF AIM

By [EXACT DATE], I will have in my possession [EXACT AMOUNT / EXACT OUTCOME].

In return I will give [EXACT SERVICE — what you will do, for whom, how much of it,
to what standard]. There is no such reality as something for nothing, and I intend
to pay in full, in advance.

My plan is: [3–5 concrete actions with owners and frequencies. Not aspirations.
"Ship one client project per month." "Apply to 10 roles a week with a written
90-day plan attached." "Train 5 days a week." "Save $X per paycheck, automatically,
before I see it."]

I am becoming the kind of person who [3–4 traits from Part 3 you are deliberately
installing — name them].

I now see this as accomplished. I hold this picture until I believe it, and I act
on it today, whether I am ready or not.

Signed: ______________________   Date: __________
```

**Rules Hill would enforce:**
- A **number** and a **date**, or it doesn't count.
- **What you give** must be written before **what you get**, or the whole thing is a wish.
- Begin the plan **at once, whether you are ready or not**. The plan will be wrong. Plans are supposed
  to get replaced (Principle 6).

### Step 2 — The daily ritual (two blocks, non-negotiable)

**Morning, immediately on waking (~15 min):**
1. Read the Definite Chief Aim **aloud**. Not silently. Aloud.
2. **Feel it as already true** for 60 seconds before you move on. If you feel nothing, you're reciting
   — slow down and picture one specific scene in detail.
3. Read the **Self-Confidence Formula** (Principle 2), aloud, all five points.
4. Write the **three actions** that move the plan today. Only three. They go before anything reactive.

**Night, last thing before sleep (~20 min):**
1. Read the Definite Chief Aim aloud again. (Hill's timing is deliberate — the pre-sleep window is
   when suggestion lands least defended.)
2. **The 30-minute visualization** from the Self-Confidence Formula, point two — think about *the
   person you intend to become*, not the trophy. Rehearse the **behavior**: how that person handles the
   hard conversation, the 6am alarm, the rejection. (Start at 10 minutes if 30 is unrealistic; build.)
3. On hard decisions, convene the **Invisible Counselors** (Principle 13) for 10 minutes in writing.

**Throughout the day:**
- **Guard the gate** (Principle 11 + the Seventh Evil). Audit your inputs like a diet. Whatever you let
  in repeatedly becomes what your subconscious runs on. Mute the accounts that make you feel behind.
- **Transmute** (Principle 10). When you feel charged up — restless, driven, something to prove —
  route it into the work or the training inside five minutes, before it goes into a screen.
- **Do more than you're paid for**, visibly, at least once. Every day.

### Step 3 — The weekly Master Mind (60–90 min, fixed slot)

Hill's minimum viable version:

- **3 to 6 people.** Fewer than 3 and it's a chat; more than 6 and harmony breaks.
- **A definite shared purpose**, or an explicit commitment to each other's definite aims.
- **Fixed schedule** — Hill says twice a week; weekly is realistic and works.
- **Everyone gets something concrete.** Decide what you give each member *before* you ask.
- **Harmony is enforced.** One cynic, one person who "just plays devil's advocate," and the asset is
  destroyed. Hill is unambiguous: friction consumes the thing you came for.
- Standard agenda: each person states last week's commitment → outcome → this week's commitment →
  one problem the group solves. 15 minutes each.

If you have no group: **build one from three people you already know** who are trying to do something.
It does not require impressive people. It requires committed ones.

### Step 4 — The weekly plan review (20 min, Sunday)

The single question from Principle 6: **is the plan working?**

- If yes → continue, raise the target.
- If no → *"replace it with a new plan… and so on, until you find a plan which DOES work."* Do not
  conclude anything about yourself. Temporary defeat means **the plan** was wrong.
- Write the new plan the same day. The gap between "this isn't working" and "here's the next attempt"
  is where almost everyone leaks out of the process.

### Step 5 — The monthly self-audit (30 min)

Run yourself against **the 16 symptoms of lack of persistence** (Principle 8) and **the 30 causes of
failure** (Principle 6). Score honestly. Have someone who knows you well score you too — Hill insists
on this, because the symptoms you can see in yourself aren't the ones costing you.

Track one number: **how many of the 16 symptoms were true of you this month?** That number going down
is the transformation, measured.

### Step 6 — The annual inventory (Hill's own 28 questions, condensed)

Hill closes Principle 6 with a year-end self-analysis. The essential ones:

1. Have I attained the goal I set as my objective for this year?
2. Have I delivered the best possible **QUALITY** of service, and the greatest possible **QUANTITY**?
3. Has the **SPIRIT** of my conduct been harmonious and cooperative at all times?
4. Have I permitted **procrastination** to decrease my efficiency, and if so, to what extent?
5. Have I improved my **personality**, and if so, how?
6. Have I been **persistent** in following my plans through to completion?
7. Have I reached decisions **promptly and definitely** on all occasions?
8. Have I permitted any of the six basic fears to decrease my efficiency?
9. Have I been either **over-cautious or under-cautious**?
10. Has my relationship with associates been pleasant or unpleasant — and **if unpleasant, is the fault
    partly or wholly mine?**
11. Have I **dissipated my energy** through lack of concentration of effort?
12. Have I been **open-minded and tolerant** on all subjects?
13. In what way have I **improved my ability to render service**?
14. Have I been **intemperate** in any of my habits?
15. Have I expressed, openly or secretly, any form of **egotism**?
16. Has my conduct toward associates induced them to **respect** me?
17. Have my opinions and decisions been based on **guesswork, or accuracy of analysis and thought**?
18. Have I followed the habit of **budgeting my time, my expenses, and my income**, and have I been
    conservative in these budgets?
19. How much time have I devoted to **unprofitable effort** which I might have used to better advantage?
20. How may I **re-budget my time and change my habits** so I will be more efficient during the coming
    year?
21. Have I been guilty of any conduct which my **own conscience** does not approve?
22. In what ways have I **rendered more service and better service** than I was paid to render?
23. Have I been **unfair** to anyone, and if so, in what way?
24. If I had been the purchaser of my own services for the year, **would I be satisfied with my
    purchase**?
25. Am I in the **right vocation**, and if not, why not?
26. Has the purchaser of my services been satisfied — and if not, **why not**?
27. What is my present **rating** on the fundamental principles of success?
28. *(Hill's closer)* Having analyzed this, what am I going to **do differently**, starting now?

Question 24 is the one that does the work. Answer it in writing every December.

## Part 5 — The 90-day transformation schedule

If you want a concrete on-ramp instead of "apply all thirteen at once," this is the sequence I'd run.
Hill's principles compound, so the order matters.

**Days 1–7 — Definiteness.**
Write the Definite Chief Aim. Start the twice-daily reading, aloud, without missing a day. Do nothing
else new. If you can't hold this for seven days, none of the rest will hold either — and that's useful
information, not a verdict.

**Days 8–21 — Decision + input control.**
Decide the three biggest open questions in your life this fortnight, in writing, with a date. Stop
broadcasting your plans (Principle 7). Audit and cut your inputs (Seventh Evil): unfollow, mute, and
put the phone outside the bedroom so the morning and night rituals aren't hijacked. Track how many
decisions you *reversed* — the goal is prompt decisions, slowly changed.

**Days 22–45 — Specialized knowledge + the plan.**
Name the one domain to become top-decile in. Buy the specific knowledge (course, books, a paid hour
with someone ahead of you). Build the written plan with owners and frequencies. Begin the weekly plan
review. Expect the first plan to fail — this is the phase where you practise replacing it without
drama.

**Days 46–60 — The Master Mind.**
Recruit 3–6 people. First meeting inside this window. Decide what each person gets. Set the fixed slot
and defend it. This is where the curve bends; solo effort has a ceiling that this removes.

**Days 61–90 — Persistence under load + transmutation.**
This is where it gets boring and nothing visible is happening. That is the design of the test — Hill's
whole thesis about "three feet from gold" lives here. Hold the ritual, hold the meetings, replace failed
plans, route your energy into the work rather than into consumption. Run the first monthly self-audit
at day 60 and again at day 90 and compare your count of the 16 symptoms.

**Day 90 — Review, then set the next 90.** The framework doesn't end; the aims get bigger.

## Part 6 — What holds up, what doesn't, and how to use the difference

You asked for everything, so here is the part most summaries skip. Using this book well requires
knowing which parts are load-bearing psychology and which are 1937 metaphysics. **The good news: the
mechanically effective parts are the majority, and they're the parts you actually execute.**

### Holds up — strongly supported by later research

| Hill's principle | What it's called now | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Definiteness of purpose; a number and a date | **Goal-setting theory** (Locke & Latham) | Specific, difficult goals reliably outperform "do your best." One of the most replicated findings in organizational psychology. |
| A definite plan expressed in continuous action | **Implementation intentions** ("if-then" plans) | Pre-deciding *when and where* you'll act roughly doubles follow-through vs. intention alone. |
| The Self-Confidence Formula | **Self-efficacy** (Bandura) | Belief in capability causally raises effort, goal difficulty chosen, and recovery from failure. |
| Persistence; the four steps; "no man is whipped until he quits" | **Grit / deliberate practice** | Sustained effort toward long-term goals predicts achievement beyond talent measures. |
| Master Mind; the friendly alliance | **Social accountability + network effects** | Public commitment to a specific person on a specific date is among the strongest known adherence levers. |
| "Temporary defeat means your plan is wrong" | **Growth mindset / attributional style** | Attributing failure to a modifiable strategy rather than a fixed self predicts persistence. |
| Specialized knowledge over general knowledge | **Deliberate practice; domain expertise** | Expertise is domain-specific and doesn't transfer broadly. Hill is right and most self-help is wrong here. |
| Guard against negative influences | **Environment design; social contagion** | Behaviors and norms genuinely propagate through networks. Choosing your inputs is choosing your defaults. |
| Emotionalized repetition before sleep | **Spaced repetition; sleep-dependent consolidation** | Not magic, but the timing is defensible. |
| Invisible Counselors | **Perspective-taking / "what would X do"** | Simulating another's decision procedure demonstrably breaks habitual framing. |

### Doesn't hold up — treat as metaphor, not mechanism

- **"Infinite Intelligence," the ether, thought vibration, telepathy** (Principles 12–13). No support.
  Keep the *practice* (undistracted thinking time, deliberate incubation, simulated counsel); discard
  the *physics*.
- **Attraction as a causal force.** Holding a picture in mind does not cause external events. It causes
  **attention, effort and persistence**, which cause events. This distinction is the difference between
  a tool and a trap.
- **"Thoughts cause illness / clean out your medicine chest."** Hill wrote this in 1937 and it's wrong.
  Do not use this book as health guidance. See a doctor.
- **The failure-is-a-mindset framing, applied to poverty.** Hill's system is a set of levers *within*
  your control; it does not follow that people who are poor failed to pull them. Structural factors are
  real. Hill's own list starts with "unfavorable hereditary background" and "unfavorable environmental
  influences during childhood," so he half-knew this. Use the framework on yourself; don't use it to
  judge anyone.
- **Some of Hill's biography is disputed.** Historians have questioned whether the Carnegie commission
  happened as described and several of the anecdotes. **This does not invalidate the operating
  system** — the practices work or don't on their own merits — but read the storytelling as
  storytelling.

### The one fix that makes this book strictly better

Hill's visualization instruction — *see and feel yourself already in possession* — has a documented
failure mode: **fantasizing about an achieved outcome can reduce the energy you bring to achieving
it.** Your brain partially banks the reward.

The fix (Gabriele Oettingen's **mental contrasting**, packaged as **WOOP**) keeps everything Hill asked
for and adds the missing half. Do this instead of pure visualization:

> **W**ish — your Definite Chief Aim.
> **O**utcome — vividly imagine having it. *(This is Hill's step 6, unchanged.)*
> **O**bstacle — now vividly imagine **the internal obstacle** that actually stops you. Be specific and
> unflattering. ("I check my phone the second I sit down." "I don't send the message because I'm afraid
> of looking stupid.")
> **P**lan — an if-then: *"If [obstacle], then I will [specific action]."*

Adding the O and the P turns Hill's ritual from a mood into a mechanism. It costs 90 extra seconds. Do
it.

## Part 7 — Quick reference

### The 13 principles, in one line each

1. **DESIRE** — a definite amount, a definite date, a definite price you'll pay, in writing, read aloud twice a day.
2. **FAITH** — manufactured confidence, installed by repetition, backed by an ethical constraint.
3. **AUTO-SUGGESTION** — the delivery system: repetition *with emotion*, or nothing gets through.
4. **SPECIALIZED KNOWLEDGE** — knowledge is only potential power; organize it into plans or it pays nothing.
5. **IMAGINATION** — synthetic (recombination) builds most fortunes; use it deliberately, on a schedule.
6. **ORGANIZED PLANNING** — plans fail; replace them immediately; the failure is in the plan, never in you.
7. **DECISION** — decide promptly, change slowly, keep your own counsel, show before you tell.
8. **PERSISTENCE** — a habit built from 8 ingredients via 4 steps; the field thins faster than the difficulty rises.
9. **MASTER MIND** — coordinated effort in a spirit of harmony; you become the average of your alliance.
10. **SEX TRANSMUTATION** — your strongest drive, pointed at your purpose instead of at consumption.
11. **SUBCONSCIOUS MIND** — it runs on whatever you feed it, so feed it deliberately; positive and negative can't coexist.
12. **THE BRAIN** — schedule quiet, undistracted, emotionally engaged thinking. Insight arrives there.
13. **THE SIXTH SENSE** — the Invisible Counselors; rebuild your character by deliberate composite.

### If you do only five things

1. **Write the Definite Chief Aim** with a number and a date, and read it aloud twice daily.
2. **Decide promptly, change slowly, and stop announcing.**
3. **Build the Master Mind.** Three people, weekly, harmony enforced.
4. **When a plan fails, write the replacement the same day.** Never conclude anything about yourself.
5. **Audit your inputs.** The Seventh Evil is the one Hill says is more fatal than all six fears.

### The five lines to memorize

> *"Whatever the mind of man can conceive and believe, it can achieve."*
>
> *"No man is ever whipped, until he QUITS — in his own mind."*
>
> *"Temporary defeat should mean only one thing — the certain knowledge that there is something wrong
> with your plan."*
>
> *"Riches do not respond to wishes. They respond only to definite plans, backed by definite desires,
> through constant persistence."*
>
> *"The building of alibis is a deeply rooted habit… Why not discard all alibis, and get down to
> business?"*
